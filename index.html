Normal: 21–35 days
Source: NICE CG44. Extended cycling regimens (e.g. 63-day tricycling for endometriosis management) can be entered here. PCOS toggle extends default to 35 days.

Default: 5 days (UK average). Endometriosis/adenomyosis toggle increases default to 9 days.
Normal: 3–7 days per cycle
Source: NICE CG44. Heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB) is defined as >7 days duration or >80ml per cycle. Prolonged bleeding is the primary driver of iron depletion in menstruating women.

Approximate volumes per cycle: Very light ~10ml, Light ~20ml, Medium ~45ml, Heavy ~80ml, Very heavy ~120ml+. Clot passage typically indicates Heavy or above. HMB threshold: >80ml. Source: BSH menorrhagia guidelines.
Iron intake & absorption
10mg
?
Default: 10 mg/day (average UK mixed diet). Vegetarian/vegan toggle reduces to 8 mg.
Normal intake: 8–15 mg/day. NHS recommended for women 19–50: 14.8 mg/day
Source: NDNS (National Diet & Nutrition Survey) UK. Red meat, fortified cereals, leafy greens are main sources. Vegetarian/vegan diets typically 8–10 mg/day from non-haem sources.
10%
?
Default: 10% (typical iron-deficient UK female, mixed diet). Coeliac toggle reduces to 5%. Vegetarian toggle reduces to 7%.
Normal range: 5–20%
Source: Hallberg et al, EJCN. Haem iron (meat): 15–35%. Non-haem iron: 2–8% baseline, rises to ~20% in iron deficiency. Inhibited by tea, coffee, calcium. Enhanced by vitamin C. Coeliac disease substantially impairs absorption.
0mg
?
Default: 0 mg. Set to 0 automatically when oral iron intolerance is toggled.
Typical prescribed dose: 65–200 mg elemental iron/day
Ferrous sulfate 200mg = 65mg elemental. Ferrous fumarate 210mg = 69mg. Alternate-day dosing shown to improve absorption vs daily by avoiding hepcidin rebound. Source: Moretti et al, Lancet Haematology 2015.
10%
?
Default: 10% (daily dosing). Increase to 15–20% for alternate-day dosing.
Typical range: 5–20%
Source: Moretti et al, Lancet Haematology. Daily dosing triggers hepcidin rise, suppressing next-day absorption. Alternate-day avoids this. Bisglycinate and liquid formulations may absorb slightly better. Coeliac reduces absorption further.
Starting ferritin & IV infusions
25
?
Default: 25 µg/L. Set to the patient's most recent measured ferritin result.
Optimal for menstruating women: 50–150 µg/L
Source: BSH/NICE. Lab lower limit ≈13 µg/L. Symptoms (fatigue, brain fog, poor exercise tolerance) typically appear below 30–50 µg/L even without anaemia. Post-IV infusion ferritin typically rises to 150–250 µg/L. Model uses 1 µg/L ≈ 8 mg stored iron.
IV infusions — month from start, dose in mg:

Typical NHS infusion: 500–1000 mg (Ferinject / ferric carboxymaltose, or iron isomaltoside). Raises ferritin by approx 60–125 µg/L per 1000 mg in absence of ongoing significant loss. Source: Ferinject SPC / NICE CG189.
