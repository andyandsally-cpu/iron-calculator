Source: NICE CG44. Heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB) is defined as >7 days duration or >80ml per cycle. Prolonged bleeding is the primary driver of iron depletion in menstruating women.

Iron intake & absorption

How this works: Enter what goes in (diet quality, supplement dose, habits). The model calculates what must be absorbed based on your ferritin trend. When you add real blood results, absorption is derived from them — becoming a diagnostic signal rather than a guess.

Source: NDNS UK. NHS recommended intake women 19-50: 14.8 mg/day. Vegetarian/vegan toggle adjusts downward as non-haem iron is less bioavailable. Dietary iron contributes ~5-15mg net to stores per month depending on deficiency state.

Elemental iron per day — not tablet weight. Ferrous sulfate 200mg = 65mg elemental. Ferrous fumarate 210mg = 69mg. Ferrous bisglycinate varies — check label. Set 0 if not supplementing.
Typical prescribed elemental dose: 65-200 mg/day
Alternate-day dosing (every other day) avoids hepcidin rebound and can give better net absorption than daily dosing. Source: Moretti et al, Lancet Haematology 2015. Set 0 automatically when oral iron intolerance is toggled.

Default: 25 µg/L. Set to the patient's most recent measured ferritin result.
Optimal for menstruating women: 50–150 µg/L
Source: BSH/NICE. Lab lower limit ≈13 µg/L. Symptoms (fatigue, brain fog, poor exercise tolerance) typically appear below 30–50 µg/L even without anaemia. Post-IV infusion ferritin typically rises to 150–250 µg/L. Model uses 1 µg/L ≈ 8 mg stored iron.
IV infusions — month from start, dose in mg:

Typical NHS infusion: 500–1000 mg (Ferinject / ferric carboxymaltose, or iron isomaltoside). Raises ferritin by approx 60–125 µg/L per 1000 mg in absence of ongoing significant loss. Source: Ferinject SPC / NICE CG189.
